# Landing page inspiration

This list covers clients from any industry and any region. It is for an agency, so do not assume the client is a SaaS product.

## How to use
1. **Read the client.** Write down five facts before you pick:
   - Industry and what they sell (product, service, place, cause, person).
   - Who buys, and where (country, language, device). Most traffic in India, Southeast Asia, Africa and Latin America is on mid-range phones.
   - The one action the site must drive: buy, book, sign up, call, visit, donate, apply.
   - Tone: calm, technical, playful, premium, warm, bold, editorial.
   - What the client already has: logo, photos, video, product shots, brand fonts.
2. **Pick the group** that matches the industry. If the client sits between two groups, use the one that matches the main action (a restaurant that sells online uses E-commerce for the shop and Food for the rest).
3. Pick **one** primary reference. You can take one detail from a second reference. Do not mix more than two.
4. If nothing fits, search one gallery from "Find more" for the industry. curated.design and Land-book filter by industry. Pick a live site.
5. Open the reference in a headless browser. Take screenshots of the hero, one content section, the conversion section (pricing, shop, booking, contact) and the footer. Check the phone layout too. Do not work from memory. Many brand sites block bots (403). Use `web-fetch-fallback` or Claude in Chrome for those.
6. Tell the user which reference you picked and why, in one or two lines.
7. Hand the reference and screenshots to `impeccable`. Copy the structure, rhythm and quality bar. Do not copy the reference's text, logo, product name, photos or illustrations.
8. For one section only, motion ideas, or fonts, colours and logos, use the reference lists in `ui-design` (`references/section-component.md`, `motion.md`, `type-brand.md`).
9. Apply `ui-rules`.

## Rules
- Do not let the page grow with every feature or service. Trim when it gets long. One section for each main job.
- Show what makes the client different. If the page never mentions it, it fails.
- The main action (buy, book, call, donate) is visible in the first screen and again at the end.
- Use the client's real photos, prices and counts. A stock-photo site loses to a plain site with real photos.
- Add a subtle background animation and small motion. Keep it clean. Luxury and editorial sites move slowly; consumer and playful sites can move more.
- Awwwards-style motion is for one detail. A business site must stay fast and clear, most of all on mid-range phones and slow networks.
- Local clients: put address, map, hours, phone and WhatsApp (where people use it) where people can find them in one tap.
- Many languages: leave room for longer text (German, Finnish) and for right-to-left layouts (Arabic, Hebrew). Check local currency, date and phone formats.
- No personal details of the agency team on a client page without consent.
